Jamaican Cultural Icon

Basic Details About Her

Lifespan: 86 Years (Sept 7, 1919 – July 26, 2006)
Known As: “Miss Lou” • Mother of Jamaican Culture
Birthplace: Kingston, Jamaica
Professions: Poet, Storyteller, Folklorist & Performer
Language: Jamaican Patois (Patwa) Champion
Famous Work: “Ring Ding” TV Show & Dialect Poems
1

About Miss Lou

  • Louise Bennett-Coverley was a beloved Jamaican poet, storyteller, and performer born on September 7, 1919 in Kingston.
  • Jamaicans lovingly called her “Miss Lou.”
  • She proudly wrote and performed in Jamaican Patois (Patwa), showing everyone that Jamaican speech is a rich and beautiful language.
  • She preserved Jamaican folk songs, proverbs, and traditional Anansi spider stories for future generations.
  • For her grand service to Jamaica, she was awarded the Order of Merit (OM)—one of the nation's highest national honors!

Children in Bandana & Why We Wear It

Bandana Plaid - National Costume

Jamaican children wearing red, yellow & white bandana plaid at Miss Lou Square in Gordon Town!

  • Ring Ding TV Show: Miss Lou hosted a famous television show where children sang songs and performed poems wearing traditional bandana plaid outfits.
  • Why Children Wear Bandana:
    • To celebrate Jamaica Day, Heritage Week, and school cultural events!
    • To feel proud of Jamaican history and African heritage.
    • To keep Miss Lou’s joyful spirit alive through song, dance, and poetry!
  • Miss Lou taught children that wearing bandana plaid and speaking Jamaican Patois was something to be truly proud of.
3

Her Lasting Legacy

  • Miss Lou passed away on July 26, 2006, but her legacy lives on in every school, song, and poem in Jamaica.
  • ⭐ Her Message to Kids: Always love, respect, and celebrate who you are and where you come from!
  • She inspired generations of artists, singers, and writers across the Caribbean and around the world.
